The Case for Experience

Institutional Knowledge

"Institutional Knowledge": the collective memory of an organization, encompassing all the documented facts, unwritten processes, history of decision-making, and cultural nuances learned by over time.

It is a critical asset that helps the commission make informed decisions, maintain operational efficiency, and prevent the repetition of past mistakes.

This shared understanding generally consists of two primary forms:

Explicit Knowledge Tangible, easily documented information like employee handbooks, standard operating procedures, historical data, and client files.

Implicit Knowledge Intangible wisdom that lives in the minds of our commission, such as intuition, unwritten workplace rules, interpersonal relationships, and hands-on problem-solving skills.

Because implicit knowledge is deeply personal, it is often considered the most vulnerable type of institutional knowledge. If seasoned staff leave without passing this information on, organizations risk losing their competitive edge and operational continuity.
